## 1. What Rockstar's official "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material" says (videos, monetisation, GTA VI-specific rules)

### Takeaway
One Rockstar policy covers every Rockstar game. It says Take-Two "does not object" to fans using its material for **non-commercial** purposes that do not intentionally spoil the plot. "Non-commercial" is defined to exclude making money from the footage, so monetised videos are **not explicitly permitted**, only tolerated in practice. No GTA VI-specific video policy had been published as of mid/late Nov 2026. The policy also lists removal triggers: ending/spoiler videos, isolated cutscenes, and footage from leaked copies before release.

### Cited Findings
- Official page title and current URL: "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material", Rockstar Games Customer Support. It covers all Rockstar games and does not name GTA 6 — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material) (an older URL, /articles/115009494848/, also exists; neither could be fetched directly).
- Core wording (from the search extract of the official page): "Take-Two Interactive does not object to our fans using materials for non-commercial uses in a manner which does not intentionally spoil the plot for others." The policy is aimed at occasional, non-commercial use of in-game content by individual members of the fan community — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-rockstar-games-material); same quote in [Tbreak](https://tbreak.com/gta-6-spoilers-rockstar-video-takedown-policy/) and [OpenWorldDaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/).
- Definition reported by secondary sources: "'Non-commercial' means that you don't make money through the game footage you post or use the material as part of a promotion for a product or service." — [OpenWorldDaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/); [Nanobananavideo](https://nanobananavideo.com/blog/gta-6-clips-rules-monetization)
- Monetisation: secondary guides read the policy as giving **no explicit monetisation licence**. Some say that read literally it means "no". Others say monetised GTA channels and Let's Plays have been tolerated for years, so monetised creators rely on Rockstar's tolerance rather than a licence — [Copyrightshark](https://copyrightshark.com/gta-6-copyright-rules/); [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/gta-6-streaming-rules); [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-monetization). One guide says Take-Two has not in practice pursued monetised Let's Play/commentary content on YouTube. This is a single, advocacy-style source — [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-monetization).
- Spoilers (as reported from the policy): "posting video showing the ending of the game, an edited combination of cutscenes and/or in-game plot dialogue, or any other big reveal in the story… any posting of these in isolation will be flagged for removal." Commentators read this as context-dependent: a cutscene inside a long playthrough is fine, a standalone cutscene upload is not — [Tbreak](https://tbreak.com/gta-6-spoilers-rockstar-video-takedown-policy/); [EZG](https://www.ezg.com/blog/gta-6-spoilers-rockstar-video-policy); [Bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/articles/gta-6-spoiler-video-rules)
- Leaks (as reported from the policy): "Any posting of in-game footage from leaked copies of the game prior to its official release date will be taken down", regardless of how the game was obtained — [Tbreak](https://tbreak.com/gta-6-spoilers-rockstar-video-takedown-policy/); [OpenWorldDaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/)
- Other removal triggers listed by secondary guides: in-game TV/comedy shows posted on their own, and unauthorised mods — [Copyrightshark](https://copyrightshark.com/gta-6-copyright-rules/); [Viceforge](https://viceforge.app/gta-6-news/gta-6-radio-music-copyright-claims)
- Out of scope of the policy: exploitation of Rockstar IP in film, TV or streaming distribution, music videos, advertisements, and publication in books, magazines or digital publishing. These need a licence, reviewed case by case; questions go to copyright@take2games.com — [Tbreak](https://tbreak.com/gta-6-spoilers-rockstar-video-takedown-policy/); [Guide GTA 6](https://guidegta6.com/en/copyright/)
- Rockstar "reserves the right" to take material down "at any time", case by case — [OpenWorldDaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/); [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-monetization)
- Last update of the page is reported as **10 January 2025**. This could not be confirmed on the official page itself — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/gta-6-streaming-rules); [Softonic](https://en.softonic.com/articles/rockstars-spoiler-takedown-warning-resurfaces-ahead-of-gta-6)
- "There is no GTA VI-specific video policy as of November 17, 2026" — [Oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/gta-6-streaming-rules)
- Insider Gaming (about two weeks before launch) reported that the spoiler section "might prevent creators and streamers from showcasing GTA 6 spoilers even after the game has launched". The policy gives no date after which spoilers become "safe" — [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/rockstar-might-dmca-gta-6-spoilers/)
- After launch, Rockstar asked players on its Discord to tag spoilers. Players in New Zealand (the first region unlocked) began sharing gameplay and story spoilers — per search summary of [LevelUp](https://www.levelup.com/en/news/rockstar-games-will-take-measures-to-prevent-grand-theft-auto-vi-spoilers-and-players-will-have-to-follow-the-rules/) and [Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/gta-6-players-face-new-spoiler-rules-as-rockstar-moves-to-protect-the-game-s-story-ahead-of-launch)

### Inferences
- A UI/UX analysis video monetised through YouTube ads falls outside the literal "non-commercial" permission. In practice it sits in the same tolerated zone as the many monetised GTA commentary channels. The risk is not zero: Rockstar can remove content at any time.
- A **sponsored** segment, or using GTA VI imagery to promote a product, a course or a template (an advertisement/promotion), is clearly outside the policy.
- For a UI analysis, the spoiler rule matters: avoid HUD/menu shots that reveal story missions, endings, major characters' fates, or late-game unlocks. Prefer early-game, open-world UI (map, phone, HUD, weapon wheel, menus).

### Gaps
- Exact, current official wording (and its date) could not be read directly because support.rockstargames.com was blocked. The quotes above come from search extracts and secondary sites.
- No source found on whether Rockstar distinguishes **screenshots** from video, or on official press-kit/Newswire asset terms of use.
- No confirmed report that the spoiler clause has actually been enforced against post-launch (after 19 Nov 2026) GTA VI videos.

## 2. Reported Content ID claims and takedowns on GTA VI trailer, gameplay and leaked videos (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
Enforcement has hit three areas: (a) **re-uploads of the trailers** (Take-Two and Universal Music Group claims/strikes in Dec 2023), (b) **leaked footage** (DMCA takedowns and subpoenas in 2026), and (c) **music** (licensed songs in trailers and radio, plus third-party Content ID misuse). Leaked or pre-release footage gets DMCA takedowns, which means copyright strikes, not just Content ID claims.

### Cited Findings
- Trailer 1 (Dec 2023): Take-Two hit videos reproducing the trailer with copyright strikes across YouTube, X and TikTok, "even IGN was hit". Universal Music Group also went after videos using the Tom Petty song "Love Is a Long Road". TikTok streams were muted, possibly because of the song — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gta-6-livestreamers-tiktok-youtube-copyright-strike-1851074512); [SVG](https://svg.com/1463343/gta-6-trailer-takedown-controversy)
- TheProfessional's reaction video, which contained the **entire** trailer with audio, got a Take-Two claim. Take-Two later rescinded it, then UMG filed one over the Tom Petty clips — [SVG](https://svg.com/1463343/gta-6-trailer-takedown-controversy). (This is the Trailer 1 era, Dec 2023, not Trailer 2.)
- Trailer 2 (May 2025): a re-upload of Angry Joe's reaction says audio claims cut it up, including claims for "2 SECONDS of AUDIO", and that YouTube's song-replacement tool replaced all audio. This comes from a single search-extract source; no official Take-Two statement on Trailer 2 reactions was found (per search summary, no direct link available).
- Musician/YouTubers incident: "Musician responds as countless YouTubers hit with copyright strikes on GTA 6 videos". A creator (reported as Frank El Santo) said his tracks were in Content ID, causing flags on fan-made trailer clips, said he was not filing claims manually, and urged creators to dispute them — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/musician-responds-as-countless-youtubers-hit-with-copyright-strikes-on-gta-6-videos-3430646/) (not fetched; details from search summary)
- June 2026: on 15 June 2026 a YouTube channel (@vepko1) streamed "GTA 6 Walkthrough Gameplay". It was quickly removed after a Take-Two claim, and another person's recording of the stream was also claimed by Take-Two — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/gta/news-gta-6-walkthrough-video-youtube-hit-take-two-dmca-strike); [HappyGamer](https://happygamer.com/gta-vi-gameplay-youtube-leak-take-two-copyright-claim-157112/)
- August 2026 leaks ("Cyberleek"): Take-Two filed DMCA takedown requests against posts sharing the leaked footage (footage from 18 Aug 2026). Rockstar's 26 Aug 2026 statement called the leaks "heartbreaking for our team" — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/rockstar-gta-6-leaks-grand-theft-auto-cyberleek-official-statement-2000728341); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2244659/rockstar-calls-gta-6-leaks-heartbreaking-says-game-is-nearly-finished/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/apparent-gta-6-footage-and-map-leak-as-rockstar-issues-takedowns/); [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/gta-6-gameplay-dmca/)
- Take-Two filed subpoena requests in federal courts (reported 21 Aug 2026) seeking records from Microsoft and Discord. It later added DMCA subpoenas to X and YouTube to identify alleged leakers, and a lawsuit could follow — [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/what-have-rockstar-and-take-two-said-about-the-latest-gta-6-leaks); [TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/take-two-expands-gta-6-leak-hunt-with-dmca-subpoenas/)
- Leaked-footage takedowns are generally copyright **strikes** (legal removal), not Content ID claims. YouTube: "channels that get 3 copyright strikes in 90 days are subject to termination" — [Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/blog/gta-6-leaks-youtube-dmca); [YouTube Help – copyright strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en-GB)
- Post-launch (Nov 2026): a ten-hour GTA VI livestream reportedly ended up in YouTube Content ID (reportedly via a music-distribution account) and "for a weekend it claimed, demonetised and even shut down other creators' streams of the same game". YouTube reportedly lifted some claims on appeal. These are blog reports and are **unverified** — [PokeIndie](https://pokeindie.com/blog/gta-6-youtube-copyright-claims); [X trending](https://x.com/i/trending/2124597674859262099)
- In-game radio / licensed music is described as the main practical claim risk. It can mute Twitch VODs and draw YouTube Content ID claims from music rights holders rather than from Take-Two — [Eklipse](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/gta-6-music-radio.html); [Mubert](https://mubert.com/blog/gta-6-dmca-radio-streaming-content-id); [Copyrightshark](https://copyrightshark.com/gta-6-copyright-rules/)
- Take-Two also sent a cease-and-desist to a fan account using Rockstar material in AI-generated images, and issued DMCA strikes on AI-generated fake GTA 6 videos — [GTABoom](https://www.gtaboom.com/take-two-cease-desist-gtasixjoker-ai-images-gta6); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/GTA-6-trailer-3-release-anticipated-as-Take-Two-targets-fake-AI-generated-leaks.1351256.0.html)
- Unreliable source flagged: choseno.com describes sweeping takedowns across YouTube/X/Reddit/TikTok. Its claims are not corroborated, so treat it as unverified — [Choseno](https://www.choseno.com/news/rockstar-games-enforces-global-copyright-takedowns-gta-6-leaks-2026-08-18)

### Inferences
- Highest risk: re-uploading trailers in full or near-full with original audio, any leaked/pre-release footage, story cutscenes, and clips with the radio or trailer music audible.
- Lower risk: short, silent or muted gameplay/screenshot excerpts with substantial voice-over analysis. Even these can catch Content ID claims (including erroneous third-party claims), so monetisation can be redirected or paused on a claim.
- Since GTA VI launched four days ago, the Content ID landscape for gameplay is still forming. Erroneous claims from third parties who registered gameplay are a live, reported risk.

### Gaps
- No primary source (Take-Two statement) found on Trailer 2 or Trailer 3 reaction-video claims.
- No data on TikTok-specific enforcement for GTA VI gameplay after launch, beyond 2023 muting of streams.
- Whether Rockstar/Take-Two itself has registered GTA VI gameplay in Content ID is unknown.

## 3. GTA VI logo (trademark) and the Pricedown font licence

### Takeaway
The GTA VI logo, the "Grand Theft Auto" name and related marks are Take-Two trademarks (several GTA VI logo applications filed). Fan sites say the fan policy does not grant logo use for merch or ads. **Pricedown** is a separate font by Ray Larabie (Typodermic, 1997). Its free style, "Pricedown Black", is reported to be free for commercial desktop use including rendered video, but the font licence grants no rights to Rockstar's logo or trademarks.

### Cited Findings
- Grand Theft Auto, GTA, Grand Theft Auto VI, the GTA VI logo, and character/place/vehicle names are trademarks and/or copyrighted works of Take-Two Interactive Software, Inc. (as stated by fan sites) — [Guide GTA 6](https://guidegta6.com/en/copyright/)
- Take-Two filed trademark applications for several GTA VI logo versions, including the full logo with "Grand Theft Auto" text and the palm-tree "VI". One application was briefly marked "abandoned" and then revived — [Trademark Lawyer Magazine](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/gta-vi-the-trademark-strategy-behind-gamings-biggest-launch/); [GTAVice.net](https://www.gtavice.net/news/why-a-gta-vi-trademark-application-was-marked-abandoned-and-how-take-two-fixed-it)
- Trademark Lawyer Magazine: commercialising derivative works without authorisation could expose creators to copyright claims "as well as potential trademark infringement or passing-off issues" — [Trademark Lawyer Magazine](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/gta-vi-the-trademark-strategy-behind-gamings-biggest-launch/)
- Merch and ads are not covered by the fan policy. Licensing requests go to copyright@take2games.com — [Guide GTA 6](https://guidegta6.com/en/copyright/)
- Pricedown history: released by Ray Larabie in 1997. The original free style was renamed **Pricedown Black**, and newer weights are paid. "Deluxe Ducks", a modification of Pricedown, is public domain — [Typodermic – Pricedown story](https://typodermicfonts.com/between-the-lines/stories/pricedown/); [Typodermic – public domain](https://typodermicfonts.com/public-domain/)
- Typodermic free desktop licence (search extract): one licensed natural person may install the font and use it for commercial and non-commercial finished work. A desktop licence "covers text rendered into video, film, broadcast graphics, and motion graphics when the distributed result does not contain the font software." No server/shared/cloud installation, no modification of the font file, and no web embedding (webfont requires a paid MyFonts licence) — [Typodermic – licensing](https://typodermicfonts.com/license/); [Typodermic Desktop EULA 2026 (PDF)](https://typodermicfonts.com/assets/documents/Typodermic%20Desktop%20EULA%202026.pdf); [Typodermic – free desktop fonts](https://typodermicfonts.com/desktop/)
- Listing sites say Pricedown Black is free for commercial use under the Typodermic Desktop License (logos, branding, advertising, title cards, rendered video), but **not** for embedding in apps, games, SaaS, editable templates, cloud design platforms or PDFs with embedded fonts — [DaFont](https://www.dafont.com/pricedown.font); [1001 Fonts](https://www.1001fonts.com/pricedown-font.html); [FontSpace](https://www.fontspace.com/pricedown-font-f19482)
- In a 2025 DaFont comment, the author said Pricedown Black can be used for a logo in Europe and the USA — [DaFont comments](https://www.dafont.com/font-comment.php?file=pricedown)
- Conflict: vectordad.com lists the font as "Free for personal use" while also saying "100% free for commercial use", and Creative Fabrica sells a commercial licence. The authoritative text is Typodermic's EULA — [Vectordad](https://vectordad.com/fonts/pricedown/); [Creative Fabrica](https://www.creativefabrica.com/product/pricedown/)
- The GTA VI logo itself is reported to be a new design (palm-tree "VI"), not plain Pricedown. Pricedown is associated with earlier GTA logos — [Trademark Lawyer Magazine](https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/gta-vi-the-trademark-strategy-behind-gamings-biggest-launch/); [Designyourway](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/grand-theft-auto-logo/)

### Inferences
- The font licence and the trademark are separate. Typing "GTA VI" in Pricedown Black may be fine font-licence-wise for rendered video, but imitating the official GTA logo/trade dress to brand a thumbnail could raise trademark or passing-off concerns, especially implying endorsement.
- For AG Studio's rules: CLAUDE.md already says logos only from supplied files, never redrawn, otherwise the name as text. Writing "GTA VI" in the channel's own font (IBM Plex) is the cleanest option. Pricedown is unnecessary and also clashes with the house style.

### Gaps
- Pricedown's own download page and the EULA could not be opened directly (typodermicfonts.com blocked). Licence terms come from search extracts.
- No source found on a Take-Two trademark on the Pricedown font itself (none is expected, but this is unverified).
- No official Rockstar guidance on nominative/referential use of the GTA VI logo in commentary thumbnails.

## 4. Practical safe options for a commentary/education (UI/UX analysis) video

### Takeaway
The lowest-risk pattern combines: your own legally obtained gameplay captures (post-launch, early game, no story spoilers), **game audio muted** (no radio/trailer music), short excerpts each framed by real analysis, no leaked or pre-release material, no full trailer re-uploads, and the name "GTA VI" in plain text instead of the logo. Even then, YouTube says only courts decide fair use, and Rockstar keeps the right to remove content, so a claim remains possible.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube: only courts can decide fair use; automated systems cannot. Courts weigh four factors, including how much of the original is used — [YouTube Help – Fair use](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en)
- YouTube lists commentary and criticism as common fair-use examples. Uses that substitute for the original are unlikely to qualify. Crediting the owner or writing "no infringement intended" does not make a use fair — [YouTube Help – Fair use FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6396261?hl=en)
- Content ID disputes: submitted in YouTube Studio. The claimant has 30 days to respond, otherwise the claim expires. The claimant may instead file a takedown, which means a strike; a counter-notification is possible. Giving credit is an invalid dispute reason — [YouTube Help – Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454?hl=en)
- A Content ID claim can monetise the video for the rights holder, block it in some regions, or just track it. It is not automatically a strike — [YouTube Help – Learn about copyright claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en); [Viceforge](https://viceforge.app/gta-6-news/gta-6-radio-music-copyright-claims)
- Remedy for music claims: remove/replace the claimed song (YouTube editor) or re-upload an edit without it — [YouTube Help – Fair use FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6396261?hl=en)
- Practitioner advice: mute licensed radio music. Most strikes on GTA videos come from music owners, not Take-Two — [Copyrightshark](https://copyrightshark.com/gta-6-copyright-rules/); [Eklipse](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/gta-6-music-radio.html); [VGKami](https://vgkami.com/gta-6-music-in-videos-and-streams-whats-the-safer-choice/)
- Commercial edge cases (sponsorships, ads, merch) need a licence request: copyright@take2games.com — [Guide GTA 6](https://guidegta6.com/en/copyright/); [OpenWorldDaily](https://openworlddaily.com/guides/gta-6-footage-streaming-policy/)

### Inferences
- **Show:** own captures of HUD, minimap, phone UI, menus, weapon wheel, map screen; brief clips (a few seconds each) with on-screen annotation and voice-over or text analysis. This favours "transformative" commentary.
- **Avoid:** trailer clips with their music (Tom Petty / licensed songs were claimed by UMG), full trailers, any leaked/pre-release (Aug 2026 "Cyberleek") footage, cutscenes or story beats, in-game TV shows, radio audio, sponsored segments around GTA imagery, the official logo redrawn or imitated.
- **Alternative that sidesteps footage risk altogether** (and fits AG Studio's "fictional, generic interfaces" rule): recreate the UI patterns as generic, fictional mock-ups in the house style, and refer to GTA VI by name only. This also avoids Content ID matching entirely.
- On TikTok/Shorts, short muted clips with commentary are the norm. Muting game audio also aligns with the AG Studio rule (no music, SFX only).
- Monetisation: expect it to work in most cases, as the many monetised GTA channels suggest, but it is tolerated, not licensed. A Take-Two or third-party claim can redirect revenue.

### Gaps
- No official Rockstar statement specifically on educational/analysis videos or on screenshots in thumbnails.
- Fair use is a US doctrine. The creator's audience and jurisdiction (e.g. Arabic-speaking regions, EU) may have different exceptions (e.g. quotation/criticism). Not researched.
- TikTok's own copyright/claim system for game footage was not specifically researched.
